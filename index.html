MSc Dissertation · c.16,000 words

Privacy Nutrition Labels for UK Web Users

Reading every privacy policy you meet in a year would take around 244 hours, so most people don't. I built a browser extension that uses an LLM to read a website's privacy policy and turn it into a standardised "nutrition label", with every claim linked back to the part of the policy it came from. Tested on four live policies, its labels agreed with a manual reading on 23 of 24 criteria.

  • Design Science Research
  • LLM
  • Browser extension
  • UK GDPR
  • Surveys & interviews
More info

Designing, Evaluating, and Publishing an Evidence-Referenced Website Privacy Nutrition Label for UK End Users

  • The problem: online consent relies on people reading privacy policies, and that system is broken. Most people agree without reading, and tools run by volunteers, like ToS;DR, can't keep up
  • Research: reviewed research on privacy policies, GDPR requirements and LLM reliability studies. LLM tools can automate this, but show error rates of around 16%, so a fixed approach grounded in evidence was needed
  • Method: Design Science Research, with a survey of 25 users and interviews with 3 extension users, developed through iterative design phases
  • Design: every policy is scored against the same fixed GDPR rubric so labels are comparable. Risky findings get extra friction, and missing information is shown honestly rather than guessed
  • Safeguards: a check that verifies each clause, to catch hallucinated claims, and cryptographic consent snapshots
  • Evaluation: tested on four live policies against a manual reading. The labels agreed on 23 of 24 criteria, and every quoted clause matched the policy word for word. All three test users rated the label highly for clarity, and pointed to sideloading as a barrier to adoption
  • Outcome: delivered a working extension, and recommended a seeded error trust study and entailment checking as next steps
  • Now: it works, but free LLMs aren't reliable enough yet for a public release. A walkthrough is available on request

BSc Dissertation · c.10,000 words

A Data Protection Framework for UK SMEs

The UK has around 5.6 million small and medium businesses, and existing frameworks are often too costly or complex for them. I built a free website that turns UK GDPR obligations into clear guidance, templates and training they can actually use.

  • Mixed methods
  • UK GDPR
  • DPA 2018
  • Surveys & interviews
  • GitHub Pages
More info

Data Protection Strategies: Creating an Actionable Framework for Improving Data Protection Compliance in Small to Medium Sized Enterprises (SME) in the United Kingdom

  • Research: critically analysed UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 against SME obligations, including how hard principles like Article 5 are to apply with limited resources. Found a clear gap in guidance written for SMEs
  • Method: mixed methods. Surveyed around 50 SMEs, interviewed 2 SMEs and a web developer, and built the framework in three iterative phases
  • Design: free templates, guides and training resources, hosted on GitHub Pages with no tracking or data collection. Designed to complement legal advice, not replace it
  • Evaluation: SME and expert feedback confirmed it was clear, relevant and practical. Suggested improvements included FAQs, more depth and interface upgrades, and developer input improved link behaviour and image loading
  • Limitations: a small sample and no data on performance over time
  • Recognition: presented the poster at GradEx, where it was selected for display at the 8th International Conference on Emerging Technologies in Computing (IAER), 2025
